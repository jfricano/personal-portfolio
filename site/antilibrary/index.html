The anti-library

Everything I haven’t
gotten to yet.

Umberto Eco kept a library of about thirty thousand books. Visitors tended to ask how many of them he had read. Nassim Nicholas Taleb, telling the story at the start of The Black Swan, argues that this is the wrong question. The books you have read are worth less than the ones you haven’t, because the unread ones mark the edge of what you know. He called that collection an anti-library.

This is mine. It started with the books on my own shelf, Slaughterhouse-Five first among them, and grew outward through the writers, ideas, films and records they led to. It is meant to stay unfinished. Search it, wander a shelf, or let it pick something for you.
