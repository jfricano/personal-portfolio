The anti-library

Everything I haven’t
gotten to yet.

Umberto Eco kept a library of about thirty thousand books, and visitors kept asking how many he had read. Nassim Nicholas Taleb, telling the story in The Black Swan, called that the wrong question: the unread books mark the edge of what you know, and the shelf should grow as you learn. He called it an anti-library.

Seneca, nearly two thousand years earlier, warned the other way. Roam through too many authors and the mind never settles, because “everywhere means nowhere.” Read a few, deeply, and keep one thought from each.

Both are right, about different things. This list is the horizon: the books, films, shows and records I haven’t gotten to, meant to outgrow any lifetime and never meant as a to-do list. The core shelf is Seneca’s answer, a dozen books to return to for life, and it should stay small. In between, I take things off the horizon one at a time and try to keep the one thought worth keeping. Search it, wander a shelf, or let it pick something for you.
