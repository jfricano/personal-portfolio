01 / The question

What if the log
came first?

I started with a practical frustration: losing workout records in an app I depended on. The replacement needed to save each set as I entered it, remain useful offline, and give me a way to take the data elsewhere.

GetFit is personal software with a focused job. It suggests the next workout, records what I actually do, and keeps the difference visible. The interface is plain because the work is in the planning and the record.

02 / The decisions

The Accordion method.
A rhythm you can read.

The generator creates four-week blocks: three loading weeks followed by a deload. This is the foundation of Accordion Training, the method at the center of bellos. Heavy, moderate, and light sessions rotate across the lifting days, with different rep ranges and more exercise variety on lighter days. Like an accordion’s bellows, heavier sessions close in to fewer big lifts and lighter sessions open out to more variety. The deload reduces the planned work.

Equipment availability shapes exercise selection. Repeated movements provide continuity, while the planner can rotate selections when logged history indicates a stall. Each workout carries a short rationale, so its structure is visible rather than hidden behind a score.

Progression comes from logged work. The next version calls this rule Earned Load: a heavier target follows evidence in the log. The generator’s TypeScript rules remain available in the original open-source project for someone to inspect and adapt.

03 / What I built

Suggestions above.
Records underneath.

The workout screen separates planned sets from actual entries and can show previous loads at matching rep ranges. Logged history feeds progression suggestions, including different handling for added weight and assistance. Calendar and history views make it possible to inspect the plan alongside the work recorded.

React and TypeScript provide the interface. The planning logic lives separately from the screens. Dexie stores records in the browser’s IndexedDB; a Hono API and SQLite provide the sync layer. A PWA service worker supports the app’s offline use, and JSON import and export keep the records portable.

04 / Make it yours

Personal by origin.
Open to adaptation.

I built GetFit around my own needs, rather than a general service with accounts and subscriptions. Its public repository carries an MIT license, so another person can reuse, modify, and build on the code.

The exercise catalog, equipment profile, and generation rules give someone a starting point for their own version. The value is both a working application and a readable implementation of the decisions that produce a workout.

05 / The adaptation

And then
I adapted it.

GetFit answered a personal need. bellos is the commercial next step. I’m developing the same planning foundation into a product for other people, with their own accounts, equipment, and records.

The product plan keeps the training engine separate from the commercial app. The original GetFit source stays available under MIT; the commercial product is being designed with its own experience and service around it.

The name keeps the sound of an accordion’s bellows and drops the w. That small edit reflects the design direction: simplicity, clear choices, and room to concentrate on the workout.

06 / Beside the bench

A phone beside you.
A quiet place to log.

This is a phone-first web app because that is where it is used: beside you in the gym, between sets. The next movement, the previous numbers, and the place to record a set need to be easy to reach and read at a glance.

The bellos design calls for larger weight and rep fields, primary actions within thumb reach, a focused workout screen, and a clear distinction between the plan and the work logged. Offline logging carries forward from GetFit. Feedback and account tasks belong outside the middle of a set.

07 / Where it stands

An open starting point.
A product taking shape.

GetFit is live, with its planner, logging, calendar, history, exercise library, and data export implemented. The screenshot above shows that v1 app at phone width.

bellos is in development. The product architecture and design describe the next version; its new interface is not shown here yet. This case study and its screenshot will be refreshed when the running version is ready.

The original app remains available at getfit.orcasolutions.dev, and its source remains open for someone who wants to run or adapt their own version.
